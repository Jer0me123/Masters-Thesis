\chapter{Evaluation}
\label{chp:evaluation}

This chapter presents the empirical results of the analyses described in the methodology. The evaluation proceeds in four stages. First, the demographic distributions of the annotated Re-LAION-5B and Stable Diffusion v1.5 corpora are characterised at both aggregate and profession level. Second, the dataset classification results from the spurious feature transformation pipeline are reported and interpreted, with per-class accuracy attribution used to localise the source of discriminative signal within each transformation. Third, the demographic probing results are presented, assessing whether gender and skin tone can be inferred from low-level image representations independently of semantic content. Fourth, the debiased generation corpus is evaluated against the aggregate Stable Diffusion v1.5 results to assess whether the inference-time interventions produce measurable demographic shifts. The chapter concludes with a unified discussion of findings, their implications, and the limitations of the evaluation.

\section{Demographic Distribution Analysis}
\label{sec:demographic_distributions}

\subsection{Aggregate Gender and Skin Tone Distributions}

At the aggregate level, both corpora exhibit clear demographic imbalance, although the form and severity of this imbalance differ between gender and skin tone. For gender, both datasets show a pronounced male skew across the full 200-profession corpus, with the imbalance substantially more severe in Stable Diffusion v1.5 than in Re-LAION-5B. Re-LAION-5B contains 83,003 female-labelled images (41.5\%) and 116,997 male-labelled images (58.5\%), corresponding to a 17 percentage-point male--female gap. Stable Diffusion v1.5 widens this gap to 41.96 percentage points, producing only 58,037 female-labelled images (29.02\%) compared with 141,963 male-labelled images (70.98\%). The generative model therefore does not merely reproduce the gender distribution observed in the retrieved corpus, but amplifies it substantially, yielding outputs that are considerably more male-skewed than the LAION-derived web-scraped imagery underlying its training distribution. This gender comparison is visualised in Figure~\ref{fig:gender_dist_aggregate}.

The skin tone results show a different form of imbalance. Rather than a binary skew, both datasets are overwhelmingly concentrated in the Mid bin (MST 4--7), with Re-LAION-5B at 86.74\% and Stable Diffusion v1.5 at 76.99\%. Within this Mid-bin concentration, however, the modal values shift toward lighter tones in the generated corpus: Re-LAION-5B peaks at MST 5 and MST 6, whereas Stable Diffusion v1.5 peaks at MST 4 and MST 5. The more pronounced divergence appears at the extremes of the scale. Re-LAION-5B has a Light-bin proportion of 12.45\% and a Dark-bin proportion of 0.81\%, indicating that non-Mid predictions are distributed, albeit unevenly, across both ends of the tone scale. Stable Diffusion v1.5, by contrast, increases the Light-bin proportion to 22.87\% while reducing the Dark-bin proportion to only 0.14\%, an 83\% reduction relative to Re-LAION-5B. MST values 9 and 10 are effectively absent in both datasets, with Stable Diffusion v1.5 generating near-zero proportions even at MST 8. This near-complete collapse of dark skin tone representation, combined with the shift toward lighter tones within the Mid bin, suggests that the model has absorbed a strong light skin tone prior from its training corpus and concentrates its outputs toward the lighter half of the tonal range. The three-bin skin tone distribution is visualised in Figure~\ref{fig:skintone_dist_aggregate}, while the full MST 1--10 distribution is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:mst_dist_aggregate}.

\subsection{Per-Profession and Per-ISCO-Group Analysis}

At the occupational-group level, gender skew is pervasive across both datasets, but its magnitude and pattern differ markedly. In Re-LAION-5B, Clerical Support is the only major ISCO-08 group to exceed demographic parity, at 58.26\% female, consistent with the historically female-dominated composition of administrative and secretarial roles. All remaining groups fall below parity, with Plant \& Machine Operators showing the strongest male skew at 77.85\% male, followed by Armed Forces at 76.5\% and Managers at 64.53\%. In Stable Diffusion v1.5, the direction of skew is broadly preserved: Clerical Support remains the only group at or near parity, at 51.93\% female. However, the magnitude of male skew increases substantially across every group without exception. The most severe amplifications occur in Skilled Agricultural, where the female proportion falls from 43.3\% to 7.3\%, Armed Forces, where it falls from 23.5\% to 7.7\%, and Plant \& Machine Operators, where it falls from 22.15\% to 13.55\%. This systematic reduction in female representation across occupational categories confirms that the amplification effect identified at the aggregate level is not driven by a small number of outlier professions, but reflects a broad structural tendency of the generative model. The ISCO-level gender distribution is visualised in Figure~\ref{fig:gender_by_isco}.

Comparing these occupational distributions against the US BLS 2024 and Global ILOSTAT reference markers further clarifies the direction of bias. Both reference baselines show broad agreement in their directional signal: groups identified as female-majority in US BLS data are also female-majority in the global statistics, while groups such as Skilled Agricultural, Craft \& Trades, and Plant \& Machine Operators are male-dominated in both. This convergence between the two independent baselines strengthens the validity of the comparison and indicates that the observed gaps are not artefacts of geographic or methodological differences between the two reference sources.

Against these baselines, Re-LAION-5B presents a mixed picture that depends on the reference used. Relative to US BLS 2024, Re-LAION-5B falls below the real-world female proportion in approximately six of ten ISCO groups, most notably in Professionals (approximately 43\% versus 50\%), Service \& Sales (approximately 46\% versus 51\%), and Technicians \& Associate Professionals (approximately 44\% versus 49\%). Against the Global ILOSTAT baseline, the picture is more balanced: Re-LAION-5B exceeds the global female proportion in several groups, including Skilled Agricultural, Craft \& Trades, and Plant \& Machine Operators, while falling below it in Professionals and Technicians \& Associate Professionals. This divergence between the two baselines reflects genuine differences in occupational gender composition between the US and global labour markets rather than inconsistency in the dataset. It suggests that Re-LAION-5B's gender distributions are broadly consistent with global workforce composition in some occupational domains while remaining below US-specific norms in others. The BLS and ILOSTAT comparisons are included in Figure~\ref{fig:gender_by_isco}.

Taken together, these comparisons establish a two-stage bias pattern. Re-LAION-5B exhibits gender distributions that diverge from real-world labour force composition in a baseline-dependent manner, falling below US-specific norms in the majority of occupational groups while remaining broadly consistent with global workforce proportions in several others. Stable Diffusion v1.5 then amplifies the male skew further across nearly every group regardless of baseline, producing occupational gender distributions that are more male-skewed than the already imbalanced retrieved corpus. The generative model therefore does not merely inherit the distributional properties of its training data, but exacerbates them, producing representations that diverge substantially from both demographic parity and empirically observed occupational gender composition.

At the individual profession level, the same amplification pattern is visible. A total of 167 of 200 professions (83.5\%) show gender skew in the same direction in both datasets, indicating that Stable Diffusion v1.5 largely inherits the directional gender associations present in the retrieved corpus. However, in 178 of 200 professions (89.0\%), the magnitude of skew is greater in Stable Diffusion v1.5 than in Re-LAION-5B. The most skewed professions illustrate this pattern clearly: Administrative Assistant, which already shows a strong female skew in Re-LAION-5B at 94.7\%, reaches 96.4\% in SD v1.5; Dietitian rises from 88.6\% to 96.9\%; and Receptionist rises from 92.0\% to 97.3\%. At the male-skewed end, the stereotyping is equally pronounced, with Logger (97.5\% male), Farmer (97.0\%), and Construction Worker (96.4\%) among the most extreme SD v1.5 outputs. The 33 professions where the datasets disagree in skew direction most likely reflect cases where the retrieved-corpus signal is weak or near parity, making generation more sensitive to prompt phrasing or random seed variation and producing nominal directional changes that do not necessarily reflect meaningful stereotyping. These cases warrant further investigation but are beyond the scope of the current analysis. The professions with the strongest gender skew are listed in Table~\ref{tab:top_biased_professions_gender}.

The ISCO-level skin tone analysis shows a similarly systematic pattern. In Re-LAION-5B, the Light-bin proportion varies narrowly between 6.9\% for Armed Forces and 13.59\% for Clerical Support, indicating that no occupational group is strongly associated with light skin tones in the retrieved corpus. In Stable Diffusion v1.5, by contrast, every ISCO group shows a substantially elevated Light-bin proportion, ranging from 16.85\% for Plant \& Machine Operators to 35.1\% for Skilled Agricultural. The full MST distribution confirms that this increase is not simply a redistribution within the Light bin, but part of a broader leftward shift in the modal MST score. Whereas Re-LAION-5B concentrates mass at MST 5--6 across occupational groups, Stable Diffusion v1.5 systematically concentrates mass at MST 4--5, with correspondingly elevated MST 3 proportions. The Dark-bin collapse observed at the aggregate level is also consistent across occupational groups: Re-LAION-5B dark-bin proportions range from 0.5\% to 1.8\%, while in SD v1.5 they approach zero in most groups, with Skilled Agricultural recording no dark-bin images. The three-bin ISCO distribution is visualised in Figure~\ref{fig:skintone_by_isco}, and the full MST group-level heatmap is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:mst_heatmap_isco}.

At the individual profession level, the scale of the skin tone amplification in SD v1.5 is particularly stark. The most light-skewed profession in Re-LAION-5B is Makeup Artist, at 27.6\% Light. In SD v1.5, the corresponding upper range is far higher, reaching 55.5\% for Carer and 56.8\% for Librarian. The SD v1.5 top ten light-skewed professions all exceed 40\% Light, a threshold not reached by any profession in Re-LAION-5B. In 175 of 200 professions (87.5\%), SD v1.5 generates a higher proportion of Light-bin images than Re-LAION-5B does for the same profession, establishing a near-universal tendency toward lighter skin tone generation that is not confined to a small subset of occupations. The professions with the highest Light-bin proportions are listed in Table~\ref{tab:top_biased_professions_skin}.

Taken together, the Light-bin increase and Dark-bin collapse are best interpreted as linked outcomes of a single directional shift in the generated skin tone distribution, rather than as two unrelated effects. The aggregate three-bin results show that SD v1.5 nearly doubles the Light-bin proportion relative to Re-LAION-5B, from 12.45\% to 22.87\%, while reducing the already small Dark-bin proportion from 0.81\% to 0.14\%. The full MST distribution clarifies this pattern: the dominant mass in Re-LAION-5B lies around MST 5--6, whereas Stable Diffusion v1.5 concentrates around MST 4--5. The ISCO-level heatmap shows the same modal shift across occupational groups, indicating that the effect is not confined to a small number of professions. The Light-bin increase therefore reflects the positive expression of this leftward shift, while the Dark-bin collapse reflects its most severe consequence at the under-represented end of the scale. Because the Dark bin is already marginal in Re-LAION-5B, even a small absolute shift away from darker tones is sufficient to reduce representation to near-zero levels in the generated corpus.

This distinction matters for downstream use. The result is not simply that Stable Diffusion v1.5 generates slightly lighter depictions on average; rather, it produces an occupational image distribution in which darker skin tones are almost absent. Any downstream application that relies on such outputs for synthetic data augmentation, occupational image corpus construction, fairness auditing, or classifier training would therefore inherit a severe coverage gap for darker skin tones. In settings where dark skin tone representation matters, this creates two risks: first, the generated corpus cannot provide meaningful visual diversity for darker-toned individuals; second, models trained or evaluated on such generated data may underestimate failure modes affecting groups that are scarcely represented in the synthetic distribution. The observed Light-bin expansion and Dark-bin collapse should therefore be read as a representational compression of the occupational image space toward lighter skin tones, not merely as a minor shift in aggregate colour statistics.

Unlike the gender analysis, a direct comparison of skin tone distributions against real-world reference statistics was not conducted. The BLS CPS data reports labour force composition in terms of self-identified racial categories rather than skin tone measurements, and converting race proportions into expected MST distributions requires an intermediate race-to-skin-tone mapping. As described in Section~\ref{subsec:race_to_mst}, this mapping was derived empirically from the FairFace dataset using the VGG-16 MST regression model. However, inspection of the resulting conditional distributions revealed that all racial groups map predominantly to the Mid bin (MST 4--7), reflecting the tonal range of the FairFace corpus rather than the full MST scale. The derived per-profession reference distributions therefore lack sufficient discriminative resolution to serve as meaningful baselines for the skin tone comparison, collapsing to near-identical Mid-dominant profiles regardless of occupational racial composition. As a result, skin tone analysis in this work is evaluated solely against the demographic parity baseline. The construction of a more granular and reliable race-to-skin-tone reference distribution, whether through a larger and more tonally diverse calibration dataset or through direct MST annotations on labour force samples, remains an open challenge and constitutes a limitation of the present analysis.

\section{Dataset Classification}

Dataset classification experiments assess whether a ConvNeXt-Tiny classifier trained on transformed image representations can distinguish Re-LAION-5B, Stable Diffusion v1.5, and COCO images as a three-class problem. Each transformation is treated as an independent experiment: images are transformed, split into training, validation, and test sets, and the classifier is trained on the transformed training set with early stopping and evaluated on the held-out test set. Chance level for all experiments is $\nicefrac{1}{3} \approx 33.3\%$ under a balanced three-class prior. Above-chance accuracy on a given transformation indicates that the transformation retains dataset-discriminating signal; the magnitude of accuracy quantifies how strongly that signal is preserved. Classification accuracy is used as the primary metric throughout this section, consistent with the three-class evaluation protocol of Zeng et al.\ \cite{Zeng_2024_UnderstandingBiasinLargeScaleVisualDatasets} from which the transformation framework is adopted.

The COCO dataset is included to extend the classification task beyond a binary comparison between Re-LAION-5B and Stable Diffusion v1.5, introducing an independent reference class and enabling class-specific diagnostic checks of whether separability is concentrated in one dataset or distributed across all three. As COCO carries no profession-conditioned annotation for gender or skin tone within this pipeline, it does not appear in the demographic analyses that follow; the results in this section therefore reflect general distributional separability across datasets and should not be interpreted as direct evidence of demographic bias, which is examined independently in Section~\ref{sec:demographic_attribute_separability}.

Table~\ref{tab:dataset_classification_results} summarises the results for all transformations evaluated. Per-class accuracy and AUC diagnostics were inspected during analysis but are not tabulated here, as they did not show substantial deviations from the aggregate pattern. The aggregate metrics are therefore sufficient for the purpose of this section, which is to characterise overall dataset separability across transformations rather than to provide a class-by-class error analysis.

\paragraph{1. The three datasets are separable across every level of representation tested}

Every transformation achieves well above the chance level of 33.3\%, with even the most aggressive information compression, mean RGB at 64.19\%, retaining substantial discriminating signal. This demonstrates that dataset-level differences between Re-LAION-5B, Stable Diffusion v1.5, and COCO persist even under extreme reduction of image content. Even when spatial structure, semantics, and most visual detail are removed, the classifier can still attribute images to their source dataset significantly better than chance.

The persistence of separability across such compressed representations suggests that dataset identity is encoded in multiple correlated visual cues rather than a single isolated artefact. These cues may include colour statistics, spatial patterns, semantic composition, or other structural regularities that differ systematically between datasets. Because these signals remain detectable even after large portions of the visual information are removed, they likely reflect broad statistical regularities rather than isolated features.

Importantly, these experiments demonstrate separability rather than its causal origins. The observed differences may arise from a combination of dataset curation practices, object distribution differences, scene composition biases, or visual statistics introduced by generative models. Consequently, the results indicate that the datasets possess distinct statistical signatures without directly identifying the specific factors responsible for those differences.

\paragraph{2. Dataset separability cannot be explained by acquisition artefacts alone}

The VAE result (99.15\%) is virtually identical to the unmodified base (99.42\%), a reduction of only 0.27 percentage points. Variational autoencoder reconstruction compresses each image through a latent bottleneck before reconstructing it, which typically suppresses certain low-level acquisition artefacts such as compression noise or encoding irregularities while preserving perceptually meaningful image structure.

Because VAE reconstruction largely preserves colour distributions, texture statistics, and spatial structure while reducing some superficial artefacts associated with file encoding or compression, the near-identical classification performance suggests that simple acquisition artefacts are unlikely to be the dominant source of discriminating signal. If dataset separability were primarily driven by compression differences, file-format characteristics, or other encoding artefacts, the VAE reconstruction process would be expected to reduce classification accuracy more substantially.

Instead, the results suggest that dataset differences are preserved in perceptual, structural, and statistical properties that survive latent reconstruction, rather than being explained primarily by superficial file-level acquisition artefacts. These may include differences in scene composition, object arrangement, photographic style, or statistical regularities introduced during dataset creation. However, VAE reconstruction does not eliminate all low-level visual statistics, such as colour distributions, texture patterns, sharpness, or local gradient structure. The result therefore indicates that acquisition artefacts are unlikely to dominate, rather than definitively ruling out all low-level contributions.

\paragraph{3. Local spatial statistics are highly dataset-discriminative}

Patch shuffling remains highly discriminative even when global layout is disrupted. With $2\times2$ patches, the classifier retains only minimal local spatial information, yet still achieves 89.91\% test accuracy. Increasing the patch size further improves performance, with $8\times8$ patch shuffling reaching 97.91\% and $16\times16$ achieving 97.69\%.

These results indicate that dataset-specific signatures are embedded in local and patch-level statistics rather than solely in coherent global scene composition. Such statistics include short-range texture patterns, local gradient relationships, colour correlations, and small-scale structural regularities. Their strong discriminative power may arise from differences in photographic style, image processing pipelines, dataset curation practices, or generative model priors. However, the experiment does not isolate the causal origin of these statistics, and the observed separability may reflect a combination of these factors.

\paragraph{4. Both frequency bands carry strong discriminating signal, with a modest advantage for high-frequency components}

High-pass filtering achieves 98.64\% test accuracy, outperforming low-pass filtering at 96.99\% by 1.65 percentage points. This small gap indicates a modest advantage for high-frequency information, such as edges, fine textures, and small-scale spatial variation. However, the low-pass result remains very high, showing that coarse illumination patterns, global shape structure, and scene layout also retain substantial dataset-discriminating signal.

Taken together, the two frequency results indicate that dataset-level differences are distributed across multiple spatial scales. Dataset separability is therefore not concentrated exclusively in either high- or low-frequency components, but persists across the frequency spectrum.

\paragraph{5. A hierarchy of dataset-discriminating signal exists across representational levels}

Table~\ref{tab:dataset_classification_results} reveals a clear hierarchy of dataset-discriminating power across the transformation suite. Unmodified and VAE-reconstructed images form the strongest tier, both achieving near-perfect accuracy ($\geq$99\%). Frequency-based and local spatial transformations follow closely: the high-pass filter and the $8{\times}8$ and $16{\times}16$ patch-shuffle variants all achieve 97--99\% accuracy, showing that dataset-specific signatures remain highly recoverable even when global layout is disrupted.

A second tier is formed by transformations that retain broader structural information. Depth and Canny edge representations achieve 96.78\% and 93.62\% accuracy respectively, indicating that scene geometry and boundary structure alone are sufficient for reliable dataset classification without colour or texture. Patch shuffle $4{\times}4$, at 94.38\%, sits within this intermediate range, retaining more local structure than $2{\times}2$ shuffling but less coherent patch-level information than the larger $8{\times}8$ and $16{\times}16$ variants.

Semantic representations form the next tier. Semantic segmentation achieves 91.36\%, caption embeddings achieve 90.99\%, and object detection achieves 88.62\%, indicating that object identity, scene semantics, and linguistic descriptions carry substantial dataset-discriminating signal, although generally less than low-level spatial and structural representations. Patch shuffle $2{\times}2$, at 89.91\%, falls close to this semantic tier, suggesting that even minimal local structure can preserve signal comparable to higher-level semantic summaries.

The weakest but still clearly above-chance tier consists of global colour statistics. Pixel shuffle achieves 80.67\%, while mean RGB achieves 64.19\%, approximately 31 percentage points above chance despite retaining only the three-dimensional average colour vector. Overall, this hierarchy indicates that dataset-discriminating information is distributed across colour, texture, spatial structure, geometry, semantics, and language. No single transformation eliminates separability, suggesting that dataset identity is not concentrated in one visual property but persists across multiple representational channels.

\paragraph{6. Semantic dataset differences survive compression to natural language}

Caption-based classification using SentenceT5 embeddings achieves 90.99\% test accuracy, despite the classifier receiving only textual descriptions rather than visual input. This indicates that dataset-discriminating signal survives compression from images into natural language.

The result suggests that differences in object co-occurrence, scene composition, and contextual relationships are reflected not only in visual statistics but also in the semantic content of generated captions. Dataset identity is therefore not confined to low-level visual patterns; it is also recoverable from linguistic descriptions of the images.

\paragraph{7. Absolute accuracies exceed those reported in the reference framework}

Across transformations shared with the Zeng et al.\ framework, the accuracies obtained here are substantially higher. However, these results are not directly comparable because the dataset configuration differs: Zeng et al.\ compare web-scraped corpora, whereas this work includes Stable Diffusion v1.5 outputs as one of the three classes.

This difference is nevertheless informative. Web-scraped corpora are internally heterogeneous because they aggregate images from many photographic sources, while Stable Diffusion v1.5 applies a consistent generative process across all outputs. This process can impose shared priors over colour, texture, spatial layout, and scene composition, producing stronger dataset-level regularities than those expected between web-scraped datasets alone. The higher accuracies observed here, together with the persistence of separability under aggressive compression, are therefore consistent with the generative pipeline being a major contributor to dataset separability. This remains an inference rather than a direct causal finding, but the pervasiveness of separability across representational levels is difficult to explain without accounting for the systematic influence of the generative process.

\section{Demographic Attribute Separability}
\label{sec:demographic_attribute_separability}

To investigate whether demographic attributes are encoded in representation channels beyond direct person appearance, a series of probe experiments were conducted using transformations designed to isolate spatial structure, semantic content, body geometry, scene context, and global colour statistics. These probes reuse the transformation-based framework introduced in the dataset classification experiments, but replace dataset identity with gender or skin tone as the prediction target. The occlusion family is particularly important because it directly tests whether demographic signal remains recoverable from scene context or mask geometry after the visible person appearance has been removed. Results are reported as AUC, with chance level at 0.500 for binary gender classification and 0.500 macro-averaged AUC for multi-class skin tone classification.

To assess result stability, each probe was trained independently across four random seeds. The reported results correspond to the selected primary run, with the remaining runs used to verify that the qualitative conclusions are stable.

For the skin tone experiments, an important label-space caveat applies. MST categories 9 and 10 are absent from both datasets, as no images in either corpus received these labels under the VGG-16 MST10 Regression annotator. This is consistent with the distributional analysis in Section~\ref{sec:demographic_distributions}, which shows near-zero Dark-bin proportions in both corpora. Since the annotator assigned MST 9 and MST 10 labels during model evaluation, their absence in the profession-conditioned image sets is more likely to reflect the extreme rarity, or complete absence, of these tones than a systematic inability of the classifier to predict them. All skin tone probing experiments therefore operate over MST categories 1--8.

\paragraph{Unmodified images establish a strong separability upper bound}

The base ResNet-50 probe achieves AUC 0.986 on Re-LAION-5B and 0.946 on SD v1.5, confirming that gender-correlated visual signal is strongly encoded in both datasets before any transformation is applied. The slightly higher value on Re-LAION-5B likely reflects the greater photographic diversity of the web-scraped corpus, which provides a wider range of appearance cues correlated with gender labels, including facial structure, body morphology, hairstyle, and clothing. These results establish an upper bound against which the reduced representations are compared and confirm that the annotation pipeline produces labels sufficiently consistent for downstream probing.

\paragraph{Occlusion probes reveal a fundamental asymmetry between datasets}

The occlusion family is the most diagnostic set of gender probes, as it directly tests whether gender information is localised within the visible person appearance or distributed across the broader scene context.

On Re-LAION-5B, all person-hidden occlusion variants collapse to chance (AUC $\approx$ 0.500), regardless of whether masking uses a bounding rectangle or a tight segmentation contour, and regardless of whether the background is retained or removed. Variation across seeds was negligible for these near-chance results, confirming that the absence of recoverable signal is not due to random initialisation. This establishes that retained scene context carries no recoverable gender signal in Re-LAION-5B. The full-person-no-background condition also yields chance performance (AUC 0.499), although this result is less straightforward to interpret: the probe collapses to majority-class prediction, which may reflect the difficulty of learning from isolated person crops under high photographic variability, annotation dependency on contextual features, or a genuine absence of learnable person-level signal in isolation. The unambiguous finding is that background context carries no independent demographic signal in Re-LAION-5B.

The pattern for SD v1.5 is qualitatively different. Both retained scene context and person-region geometry carry substantial gender signal. When the person is replaced by a white rectangle while the background is retained, the probe achieves AUC 0.747, indicating that the surrounding scene and coarse person-box geometry are strongly gender-correlated. When only the tight person-shaped silhouette is retained on a black background, performance rises to AUC 0.834, an 8.7 percentage-point increase over the rectangle-with-background condition. This indicates that the silhouette contour itself encodes gender signal beyond what is available from rectangular masking, likely through body shape and spatial proportions preserved by the tight segmentation boundary. When silhouette geometry and background context are available together under segmentation masking, performance reaches AUC 0.852, consistent with both sources contributing to the recovered gender signal. The central finding is therefore not which source is stronger in isolation, but that SD v1.5 encodes gender signal outside visible person appearance, whereas Re-LAION-5B does not. This is consistent with the contextual gender artefact identified by Meister et al.\ and shows that the effect is especially pronounced in the generated corpus.

\paragraph{Global colour statistics carry modest but consistent signal in both datasets}

Mean RGB classification achieves AUC 0.600 on Re-LAION-5B and 0.579 on SD v1.5. These values are comparable to the AUC of 0.580 reported by Meister et al.\ for mean RGB gender probing on COCO, suggesting that the magnitude of the photometric gender signal in both datasets is similar to that observed in a general-purpose image benchmark. The results indicate that gender-correlated photometric statistics remain detectable even when each image is compressed to three global colour values. These statistics may reflect systematic differences in clothing colour, lighting, background tone, or other photometric properties associated with gender labels. The similar performance across datasets suggests that this signal is not specific to either Re-LAION-5B or SD v1.5, but is a modest and recurring feature of occupational person imagery.

\paragraph{Geometric probes reveal body configuration as an independent signal source}

Pose keypoints achieve AUC 0.697 on Re-LAION-5B and 0.680 on SD v1.5, indicating that normalised skeletal geometry correlates measurably with gender labels in both datasets. Because pose keypoints encode body proportions, posture, and limb arrangement without retaining colour or texture information, this result shows that body configuration contributes to gender separability independently of photometric appearance.

Depth values sampled at pose keypoint locations yield AUC 0.559 on Re-LAION-5B and 0.592 on SD v1.5, the lowest values among the above-chance probes. Although both exceed chance, the magnitude of separation is small, indicating that sparse 3D structure at skeletal locations carries only weak gender-correlated signal. Depth at keypoints is therefore substantially less informative than pose geometry, suggesting that the spatial configuration of pose keypoints carries more gender signal than the relative depth values sampled at those same keypoints. This suggests that sparse depth structure is not a dominant source of gender signal, although pose geometry itself remains informative.

\paragraph{Semantic probes reveal scene composition and language as additional signal carriers}

Object detection probes show that scene composition and object co-occurrence carry gender-correlated information. On Re-LAION-5B, the unrestricted object detection probe achieves AUC 0.692, compared to 0.615 for the restricted variant. This 7.7 percentage-point gap indicates that objects appearing alongside people in Re-LAION-5B carry additional gender signal beyond the person-related labels. On SD v1.5, both object detection variants converge at AUC 0.608, suggesting that this form of object-level gender stereotyping is not amplified in the generated corpus to the same degree as the background signal observed under occlusion.

Caption-based probing shows that gender-correlated signal also survives compression to natural language. Captions achieve AUC 0.901 on Re-LAION-5B and 0.771 on SD v1.5, indicating that semantic descriptions of the images retain substantial gender signal in both datasets. Because the probe receives only textual input, these results show that gender associations are not confined to low-level image statistics: they are also present in the objects, activities, occupational contexts, and scene details captured by the captioning model. The stronger Re-LAION-5B caption result suggests that web-scraped images contain richer or more varied linguistic associations between gender, occupation, objects, and scene context. In SD v1.5, the caption signal remains clearly above chance, showing that gender stereotyping in the generated corpus is also reflected in semantic content, although less strongly than in the Re-LAION-5B captions.

\paragraph{The two datasets exhibit qualitatively distinct spurious gender feature profiles}

Considered jointly, the results reveal a clear structural difference between the two datasets. In Re-LAION-5B, retained background context carries no independent gender signal: all person-hidden occlusion variants collapse to chance regardless of whether the background is retained or removed. Other reduced representations, including mean RGB, object detection, and pose keypoints, carry modest above-chance signal, while captions retain strong gender signal. The full-person-no-background condition also collapses to chance, although this result is ambiguous and does not support a strong conclusion about person-level signal in isolation.

In SD v1.5, the defining feature is the presence of strong contextual gender signal. Gender is recoverable not only from visible person appearance, but also from retained scene context and person-region geometry. This pattern has no counterpart in Re-LAION-5B, where the same occlusion conditions remain at chance. Although some reduced representations, such as mean RGB, object detection, and pose keypoints, are comparable to or weaker than their Re-LAION-5B counterparts, SD v1.5 uniquely projects gender signal into the surrounding scene. This profile is consistent with the hypothesis that the generative model amplifies demographic priors from its LAION-derived training distribution, extending them beyond the generated person into background composition, spatial layout, and semantic content.

\paragraph{Unmodified images establish a strong separability upper bound}

The base ResNet-50 probe achieves macro-AUC 0.968 on Re-LAION-5B and 0.900 on SD v1.5, confirming that skin tone information is strongly recoverable from unmodified images in both datasets. The per-class results are highly consistent across MST categories for Re-LAION-5B, ranging from 0.948 to 0.980. In SD v1.5, the per-class values vary more substantially, ranging from 0.779 to 0.996, with the lowest AUCs occurring for the mid-range tones MST4 and MST5. This indicates that skin tone is less uniformly separable across the tonal spectrum in the generated images than in the web-scraped corpus, a pattern that becomes more pronounced under reduced representations.

\paragraph{Occlusion probes reveal the same dataset asymmetry observed for gender, with per-class variation}

All occlusion variants on Re-LAION-5B produce macro-AUC values at chance (0.514--0.515). The per-class breakdown confirms that this is not caused by cancellation across classes: individual AUCs for MST1 through MST7 remain at chance (0.499--0.501) under every masking strategy. The exception is MST8, which reaches AUC 0.614 consistently across all variants, including those where the background is fully removed. Because this value is stable across masking strategies, it most plausibly reflects a small-sample artefact in this category rather than genuine contextual signal.

The person-hidden variants therefore show that retained scene context carries no recoverable skin tone signal in Re-LAION-5B. As with gender, the full-person-no-background condition also collapses to chance (0.514), although this result is less straightforward to interpret. It may reflect the photographic variability of web-scraped person crops, annotation dependency on contextual features, or a genuine absence of learnable person-level signal in isolation. The unambiguous finding is that background context carries no independent skin tone signal in Re-LAION-5B, matching the pattern observed for gender.

On SD v1.5, occlusion probes retain substantial above-chance performance across all masking strategies, with macro-AUC ranging from 0.660 to 0.899. The per-class results show that this signal is strongest at the extremes of the tonal distribution. MST1 and MST8 achieve the highest per-class AUCs across all variants, including 0.876 and 0.921 under segmentation masking, while mid-range tones such as MST4 and MST5 remain substantially lower. This indicates that the generative model encodes the strongest contextual skin tone priors at the tonal extremes, where scene associations may be most pronounced.

The same occlusion gradient observed for gender is present here. Segmentation-based masking consistently outperforms rectangular masking, and retaining the background alongside person occlusion consistently outperforms removing it. This indicates that skin tone signal in SD v1.5 is distributed across both person-region geometry and retained scene context, with the background providing additional information beyond mask geometry alone.

\paragraph{Global colour statistics carry weaker photometric signal than for gender}

Mean RGB achieves macro-AUC 0.563 on Re-LAION-5B and 0.543 on SD v1.5, lower than the corresponding gender values of 0.600 and 0.579. This is consistent with the expectation that global colour statistics carry weaker signal for skin tone than for gender. Skin tone is primarily a localised photometric property of face and body regions, so averaging colour across the full image dilutes this signal. By contrast, gender-correlated colour variation may also include broader cues such as clothing, hair, lighting, and background tone.

The per-class mean RGB results for Re-LAION-5B are broadly distributed across MST categories, ranging from 0.518 to 0.586, with no clear concentration at the tonal extremes. This suggests that global colour statistics carry only weak skin tone signal, without a clear concentration in any specific MST category.

\paragraph{Geometric probes show stronger skin tone signal in SD v1.5}

Pose keypoints and depth at pose keypoints produce similar macro-AUC values within each dataset. On Re-LAION-5B, pose keypoints achieve 0.596 and depth at pose keypoints achieves 0.598. On SD v1.5, the corresponding values are higher, at 0.666 and 0.655. This indicates that body configuration and sparse depth structure carry moderate skin tone signal, with stronger encoding in the generated corpus than in the web-scraped corpus.

The per-class pose results show that, as with the occlusion probes, the strongest SD v1.5 signal is concentrated at the tonal extremes. MST1 and MST8 achieve AUC 0.770 and 0.875 respectively, while MST4 remains close to chance at 0.539. This indicates that the skin tone signal recovered from geometric representations is not uniform across the tonal scale. Instead, SD v1.5 shows stronger separability for the lightest and darkest available MST categories, while mid-range tones remain harder to distinguish. This repeated pattern across occlusion and pose probes suggests that the skin tone signal in SD v1.5 is not limited to background context. It also appears in body-configuration cues, especially at the lightest and darkest available MST categories.

\paragraph{Object detection probes show that scene composition encodes skin tone in both datasets}

Both object detection variants achieve moderate macro-AUC values across datasets. The restricted probe reaches 0.591 on Re-LAION-5B and 0.626 on SD v1.5, while the unrestricted probe reaches 0.611 and 0.663 respectively. In contrast to the gender results, where the unrestricted Re-LAION-5B probe substantially outperforms the restricted variant (0.692 versus 0.615), the gap is modest for skin tone: approximately 2 percentage points for Re-LAION-5B and 3.7 percentage points for SD v1.5. This suggests that additional object co-occurrence contributes less skin tone signal than gender signal.

The consistently higher SD v1.5 values indicate that skin tone is more strongly encoded in object-level scene composition in the generated corpus than in the web-scraped corpus. This is consistent with the broader pattern observed across the reduced visual probes, where SD v1.5 tends to retain stronger demographic signal outside direct person appearance.

\paragraph{Caption probing replicates the cross-dataset reversal observed for gender}

Caption-based probing replicates the cross-dataset reversal already observed in the gender caption results. Re-LAION-5B achieves macro-AUC 0.749, compared to 0.673 for SD v1.5. This differs from most non-caption visual probes, where SD v1.5 generally shows stronger skin tone signal than Re-LAION-5B. The same reversal appearing for both gender and skin tone suggests that demographic associations are encoded differently in the captions of real and generated images.

The Re-LAION-5B per-class caption AUCs are relatively consistent across tones, ranging from 0.719 to 0.784. This indicates that skin tone signal in captions is distributed broadly across the MST scale rather than concentrated only at the extremes. In SD v1.5, the per-class caption AUCs follow the pattern seen in several visual probes: MST1 and MST8 are more separable, while mid-range tones produce lower values. Together, these results suggest that SD v1.5 embeds skin tone priors more strongly in visual scene statistics, while Re-LAION-5B retains stronger and more evenly distributed skin tone associations in caption-level semantic content.

\paragraph{Per-class results reveal weaker separability for mid-range skin tones}

Across most reduced representations, especially in SD v1.5, MST1 and MST8 achieve higher per-class AUCs than the mid-range categories MST4 and MST5. This pattern suggests that the lightest and darkest available MST categories are linked to more distinctive non-skin cues, while mid-range tones are harder to separate from neighbouring categories. The result is important methodologically because macro-averaged AUC can obscure this per-class variation: a probe may appear moderately effective overall while still being much weaker for specific MST categories.

\paragraph{Skin tone and gender exhibit analogous but not identical spurious feature profiles}

The skin tone results largely follow the structural pattern observed for gender. In Re-LAION-5B, background context carries no independent skin tone signal, while SD v1.5 distributes skin tone signal across scene context, mask geometry, object composition, body configuration, and captions. The main differences are quantitative rather than structural: skin tone AUCs under occlusion are generally lower than the corresponding gender AUCs, mean RGB is weaker for skin tone than for gender, and the caption results show a reversal relative to the dominant visual-probe pattern. The central finding nevertheless remains the same for both attributes: SD v1.5 projects demographic signal beyond visible person appearance in a way that is not observed in the web-scraped corpus.

\subsection{Connections to Dataset Classification Results}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

Several parallels link the demographic probe experiments to the earlier dataset classification findings. Together, the two sets of results show that statistical signal is distributed across multiple representation levels rather than confined to a single image property.

The most direct parallel is the persistence of signal across transformations. In the dataset classification experiments, separability remained above chance for every transformation tested, from mean RGB at 64.19\% to caption embeddings at 90.99\%. This indicates that dataset identity is encoded across several visual and semantic channels at once. The demographic probing results show a related pattern: gender and skin tone are not recoverable from one representation alone, but appear across photometric probes, geometric probes, semantic probes, and occlusion-based contextual probes. In both analyses, the relevant signal is therefore distributed across the representational spectrum rather than being an artefact of a single cue.

The clearest connection between the two analyses concerns Stable Diffusion v1.5. In the dataset classification task, the high accuracies suggest that SD v1.5 outputs contain strong dataset-specific regularities. This is consistent with the fact that the generative pipeline applies a shared set of learned priors across its outputs. The demographic probing results provide a more targeted view of this regularity. SD v1.5 encodes gender at AUC 0.946 in the unmodified image and AUC 0.747 under background-retained rectangular occlusion; the corresponding skin tone values are 0.900 and 0.728. These results indicate that the generated corpus contains stable associations between person appearance, scene context, and person-region geometry. Re-LAION-5B shows no comparable contextual signal under person-hidden occlusion conditions, indicating that its scene context does not carry the same independent demographic signal. The full-person-no-background condition also collapses to chance in Re-LAION-5B, although this result remains ambiguous and does not support a definitive conclusion about whether isolated person appearance is sufficient in real photographs.

The caption results provide a second connection between the two analyses. Dataset identity survives compression to natural language at 90.99\% classification accuracy, placing caption-based classification close to semantic segmentation and above object detection in the dataset classification hierarchy. Demographic signal also survives in captions: gender achieves AUC 0.901 on Re-LAION-5B and 0.771 on SD v1.5, while skin tone achieves 0.749 and 0.673 respectively. Unlike the strongest occlusion results, where SD v1.5 shows the clearest contextual demographic signal, captions show stronger demographic separability in Re-LAION-5B. This suggests that web-scraped images contain richer or more varied semantic associations between demographic attributes, occupations, objects, and activities. The caption findings therefore show that stereotyping is not confined to pixel statistics or geometry, but is also present in the semantic content of the images.

Finally, the depth and edge classification results connect to the depth-at-pose-keypoints probe used in the demographic analysis. Full depth maps achieve 96.78\% dataset classification accuracy, and Canny edges achieve 93.62\%, confirming that structural representations carry strong dataset-discriminating signal. At the demographic level, however, depth sampled only at pose keypoints is much weaker: it reaches AUC 0.559 for gender and 0.598 for skin tone on Re-LAION-5B, and 0.592 and 0.655 on SD v1.5. This contrast suggests that the structural signal captured by full depth maps is driven largely by broader composition, such as camera distance, scene depth, and background structure, rather than by sparse body-depth information alone. The depth-at-keypoints probe therefore isolates a smaller demographic component of the broader structural signal while discarding much of the scene-level information that makes full depth maps highly discriminative for dataset classification.

\section{Debiasing Evaluation}
\label{sec:debiasing_evaluation}

The debiasing evaluation compares the demographic distribution and spurious feature profile of the 10,000-image debiased doctor corpus against the full Stable Diffusion v1.5 generation results reported in Sections~\ref{sec:demographic_distributions} and~\ref{sec:demographic_attribute_separability}. The evaluation uses the aggregate SD v1.5 findings as the reference point rather than constructing a separate doctor-specific baseline. This allows the debiased corpus to be assessed against the overall demographic and spurious-feature profile characteristic of standard Stable Diffusion v1.5 generation.

This comparison directly tests whether the combined ITI-GEN, ControlNet, and Chamfer colour alignment pipeline produces a measurable departure from the standard generation profile. The rationale for selecting the doctor profession is discussed in Section~\ref{subsec:debias_design}. In summary, doctor was selected because it exhibits one of the most pronounced demographic skews in the standard pipeline, carries a well-documented real-world demographic baseline, and has been used as an evaluation case in prior work on occupational bias in text-to-image generation \cite{Bianchi_2023_TTIGenAmplifiesDemographicStereotypes, Luccioni_2023_AnalyzingSocietalRepresentationsDiffusionModels}.

An important methodological distinction applies throughout this section. Demographic labels for the debiased corpus are not derived from automated classifiers, but are assigned directly from the generation configuration. Each image is labelled with the ITI-GEN gender token and MST skin tone token used to produce it. This removes annotation uncertainty as a confound and ensures that the probing classifiers measure whether the demographic conditioning signal is encoded in different representations, rather than whether an external classifier can recover predicted demographic attributes.

For gender, high probing accuracy on person-level representations is therefore an expected consequence of successful ITI-GEN conditioning rather than evidence of spurious signal. For skin tone, the 10-class task is substantially harder than the binary gender task, and person-level probe values should be interpreted relative to the macro-averaged AUC chance level of 0.500 rather than compared directly with the binary gender results.

\subsection{Demographic Distribution Shift}
\label{subsec:debias_distribution}

The debiased generation pipeline was configured to produce a balanced corpus across both gender and skin tone. ITI-GEN embeddings were applied for female and male gender categories in equal proportions across the 10,000-image corpus, while MST conditioning was applied uniformly across all ten skin tone levels, MST~1--10. This produced a target distribution of approximately 500 images per gender--skin tone combination. After structural validation using the same acceptance criteria as the standard generation pipeline, the effective composition of the debiased corpus remained aligned with this intended uniform design, subject to profession-specific rejection rates.

By construction, the debiased corpus achieves an approximately balanced gender distribution, with 50\% female and 50\% male conditioning. This contrasts directly with the standard Stable Diffusion v1.5 pipeline, which produced only 29.02\% female images across all 200 professions. As discussed in Section~\ref{sec:demographic_distributions}, that standard distribution reflects substantial amplification of gender imbalance relative to the retrieved corpus.

The skin tone distribution is also balanced by construction across MST~1--10. This contrasts with the standard pipeline, where 76.99\% of generated images fell in the Mid bin whilst Dark-bin representation approached zero across most professions. The debiased generation configuration therefore produces a distribution that is sharply different from the demographic profile observed under standard SD v1.5 generation.

\subsubsection{Gender Probing Results}

Person-level representations confirm successful demographic conditioning. The unmodified image probe achieves AUC $\approx$1.000 on the debiased corpus, while person-centred occlusion variants are also very high: 0.998 for full person appearance without background (FullNoBg), 0.999 for the segmentation-masked person region with background retained (MaskSegm), and 0.985 for the silhouette alone without background (MaskSegmNoBg). These values exceed the corresponding standard SD v1.5 results by substantial margins.

This outcome is expected. ITI-GEN directly conditions the person's visual appearance, and labels in the debiased corpus are assigned from the conditioning token rather than from an external classifier. High person-level separability therefore confirms that the conditioning pipeline produced visibly gendered images and encoded the intended demographic signal in the depicted person.

Across most non-person representations, gender signal is substantially reduced. Mean RGB falls from 0.579 on the standard SD v1.5 corpus to 0.507 on the debiased corpus, effectively at the binary chance level of 0.500. Object detection falls to 0.449 for the unrestricted vocabulary and 0.457 for the restricted vocabulary, indicating no systematic gender-correlated object co-occurrence in the debiased corpus. Depth at pose keypoints falls to 0.454, and pose keypoints fall to 0.475, both close to chance and consistent with little recoverable gender signal in geometric representations. These reductions contrast with the standard SD v1.5 corpus, where the same probes achieved AUC values between 0.579 and 0.680.

Caption-based probing falls from 0.771 on the standard SD v1.5 corpus to 0.530 on the debiased corpus, a reduction of 24.1 percentage points. This indicates that the debiasing pipeline substantially reduces gender-correlated semantic content in generated images, even though caption generation is not directly controlled by the interventions. The result suggests that the reduction in gender signal extends beyond low-level visual features and also affects the semantic content captured by captions.

The main exception is the background-retained rectangular occlusion probe. This probe achieves AUC 0.995 on the debiased corpus, indicating that strong background-level gender signal persists even when the person region is hidden. This is expected because none of the debiasing interventions directly targets background composition: ITI-GEN conditions person appearance, ControlNet constrains skeletal pose, and Chamfer alignment steers global colour statistics. Scene-level content therefore remains free to co-vary with the gender conditioning token. The corresponding standard SD v1.5 value is 0.747, making this increase particularly important. The persistence of near-person-level AUC under background-retained occlusion shows that background-level demographic conditioning remains substantially entangled with the generation process and represents the primary avenue for further improvement.


\subsubsection{Skin Tone Probing Results}

The skin tone probing results show reductions across all representations relative to the standard SD v1.5 corpus. Unlike the gender case, person-level probes decrease rather than increase in the debiased corpus: the unmodified base probe falls from 0.900 to 0.861, and the full-person-no-background probe falls from 0.899 to 0.823. This reduction should be interpreted in light of the harder label space used for the debiased corpus. The standard SD v1.5 comparison uses MST~1--8, while the debiased corpus is evaluated across the full MST~1--10 range under a balanced distribution. The person-level values of 0.823--0.861 remain substantially above the macro-AUC chance level of 0.500, confirming that ITI-GEN conditioning successfully encodes skin tone signal at the person level across the ten-point scale.

The clearest evidence for reduced global photometric signal comes from the Mean RGB probe, which falls from 0.543 on the standard SD v1.5 corpus to 0.482 on the debiased corpus. This is the skin tone probe closest to the chance level of 0.500. Since Mean RGB compresses each image to its global average colour per channel, the near-chance value indicates that the debiased corpus contains little systematic skin-tone-correlated signal in global colour statistics. This supports the conclusion that the colour alignment component disrupts the photometric correlations that previously allowed global colour statistics to carry demographic signal.

The remaining non-person probes also collapse to at or near chance. Object detection falls from 0.663 to 0.439 for the unrestricted vocabulary and from 0.626 to 0.461 for the restricted vocabulary, indicating no systematic skin-tone-correlated object co-occurrence in the debiased corpus. Depth at pose keypoints falls from 0.655 to 0.443, and pose keypoints fall from 0.666 to 0.483, both close to chance and consistent with little recoverable skin tone signal in geometric representations. Caption probing falls from 0.673 to 0.490, indicating that the semantic content captured by generated captions is no longer systematically correlated with the skin tone conditioning token.

The occlusion probes show consistent reductions across all variants, but some residual contextual signal remains. MaskRectNoBg falls from 0.660 to 0.543, close to chance, indicating that rectangular person-box geometry alone carries little residual skin tone signal after debiasing. MaskRect falls from 0.728 to 0.632, indicating a partial but incomplete reduction of background-retained skin tone signal. The remaining above-chance performance in MaskRect suggests that scene composition retains some skin-tone-correlated properties, consistent with the background entanglement observed in the gender analysis.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Several limitations qualify the interpretation of the results presented in this chapter.

\paragraph{Scope of the Re-LAION-5B sample.}
The analysis is conducted on four of 128 shards of Re-LAION-5B, representing approximately 3.1\% of the full dataset. Findings are therefore estimates of corpus-level properties rather than exact population parameters, and biases introduced during the original web-crawling and filtering process cannot be fully assessed from the released shard data alone.

\paragraph{Annotation as measurement instrument.}
Demographic labels are produced by automated classifiers rather than ground-truth human annotation. Gender predictions are binary and do not capture non-binary gender expression. Skin tone estimates are sensitive to imaging conditions and lighting variation in web-scraped data. Observed demographic distributions should therefore be interpreted as classifier-derived labels rather than definitive ground truth.

\paragraph{Absence of a real-world skin tone baseline.}
Unlike gender, skin tone could not be compared against a reliable real-world occupational baseline. The available BLS reference data reports racial categories rather than skin tone, introducing a further limitation because race and skin tone are related but not equivalent constructs. An exploratory race-to-MST calibration was attempted using FairFace as an intermediate reference corpus, but the resulting conditional distributions mapped all racial groups predominantly to the Mid bin, providing insufficient variation across MST categories for meaningful profession-level comparison. No alternative baseline was adopted because deriving population-level skin tone distributions from racial labour-force statistics would require unsupported assumptions about within-category skin tone variation. Skin tone results are therefore interpreted as relative differences between Re-LAION-5B and Stable Diffusion v1.5 rather than as deviations from a real-world skin tone ground truth.

\paragraph{Dataset classification and causation.}
Above-chance classification accuracy confirms that a transformation retains dataset-discriminating signal, but it does not establish which specific properties of each dataset produce that signal. The demographic probing experiments provide a more targeted analysis by testing whether gender and skin tone are recoverable from specific reduced representations, such as background context, pose, object detections, captions, and global colour statistics. However, these probes still identify statistical associations rather than causal mechanisms. Further controlled experiments would be required to isolate the exact image properties responsible for each source of separability.

\paragraph{Debiasing evaluation scope.}
The debiasing evaluation is restricted to a single profession and a single combination of inference-time interventions. It does not evaluate weight editing or fine-tuning approaches, and results may not generalise to other professions, prompts, or model architectures. The probing results show that spurious correlations are substantially reduced at the representation level for the doctor profession, but whether this reduction generalises across the full 200 profession set or persists under different prompt formulations remains untested. The residual signal identified under background-retained occlusion in both the gender and skin tone analyses indicates that background-level demographic conditioning remains partially entangled with the generation process and constitutes the primary open question for future work on this pipeline.

\section{Synthesis of Evaluation Findings}
\label{sec:overall_conclusions}

The dataset classification experiments establish that Re-LAION-5B, Stable Diffusion v1.5 generated images, and COCO are distinguishable with high accuracy across every level of visual representation tested, from unmodified images at 99.42\% down to mean RGB compression at 64.19\%. The persistence of above-chance separability even under the most aggressive information reduction demonstrates that dataset identity is encoded in multiple correlated visual cues rather than in any single isolated artefact. The VAE experiment further shows that this separability is unlikely to be explained primarily by low-level acquisition artefacts, as reconstruction through the Stable Diffusion latent bottleneck reduces accuracy by only 0.27 percentage points.

The demographic distribution analysis shows that Re-LAION-5B exhibits gender distributions that diverge from real-world labour force composition in a baseline-dependent manner, while Stable Diffusion v1.5 amplifies the male skew across nearly every occupational group. Gender amplification is observed in 178 of 200 professions, with the female proportion falling from 41.5\% in Re-LAION-5B to 29.02\% in Stable Diffusion v1.5. The skin tone analysis reveals a near-complete collapse of dark-toned representation in generated outputs: the Dark-bin proportion falls from 0.81\% in Re-LAION-5B to 0.14\% in Stable Diffusion v1.5, an 83\% reduction, while the Light-bin proportion almost doubles from 12.45\% to 22.87\%. Skin tone amplification toward lighter tones is observed in 175 of 200 professions.

The demographic probing experiments reveal distinct spurious feature profiles in the two datasets. In Re-LAION-5B, retained background context carries no independent demographic signal: every person-hidden occlusion variant collapses to chance regardless of whether background is retained, and residual signal from colour statistics, object co-occurrence, and skeletal geometry is modest. The full-person-no-background condition also collapses to chance, although this result is ambiguous and does not support a strong conclusion about person-level signal in isolation.

In Stable Diffusion v1.5, demographic signal is recoverable from representations that do not preserve visible person appearance. It appears under background-retained occlusion, from person-region geometry, and in natural language captions. This pattern is not observed in Re-LAION-5B, where person-hidden occlusion variants collapse to chance. The strongest difference is therefore not simply that SD v1.5 depicts gender and skin tone in the person region, but that demographic signal is also embedded in the surrounding scene and in the spatial structure of the generated image.

The caption-based results add a further distinction between the datasets. Captions retain stronger demographic signal for Re-LAION-5B than for Stable Diffusion v1.5, even though the strongest visual contextual signals occur in the generated corpus. One plausible explanation is the gender-neutral remapping applied before caption classification: generated images produce more repetitive captions, removing explicit gendered terms may remove a larger share of the available caption signal. Captions of web-scraped images, by contrast, reflect a broader range of occupational settings, activities, objects, and scene contexts, allowing demographic associations to remain recoverable even after remapping. This cross-modal persistence shows that demographic stereotyping is not confined to low-level pixel statistics, but extends across visual, geometric, contextual, and semantic representations.

The debiasing evaluation demonstrates that the combined ITI-GEN, ControlNet, and Chamfer colour alignment pipeline produces a measurable departure from the demographic and spurious feature profile of the standard generation pipeline. Most non-person probes collapse to at or near chance for both gender and skin tone, including mean RGB, object detection, pose, depth-at-pose, and caption-based representations. This indicates that the interventions substantially reduce low-level photometric correlations, object-level stereotyping, geometric signal, and semantic stereotyping in the debiased doctor corpus. However, residual signal persists under background-retained occlusion, indicating that background-level demographic conditioning remains partially entangled with the generation process even under explicit pose and appearance control.
